“We all have 24 hours in a day, yet some people achieve more than others. Why is that?”

“Time is the most valuable resource — once lost, it can never be regained.”
This presentation is about mistakes that waste our time, and simple techniques to fix them. I’ll also share some numbers in easy words to show how much time we actually lose.
23 minutes lost → One interruption takes about 23 minutes to get back to full focus.
28% of week → Almost one-third of our work week is spent just checking emails and messages.
80/20 rule → Most of our results come from a small part of our effort — so we should focus on that important part.

We waste time every day by multitasking, not knowing priorities, procrastinating, and poor planning. To fix this, we use the Eisenhower Matrix. It tells us: do urgent and important tasks first, schedule important ones for later, delegate urgent but less important tasks, and eliminate the ones that don’t matter.

The Pomodoro Technique means working in short focus sprints — 25 minutes of work, 5 minutes of break, and after four rounds, a longer rest. Time Blocking means fixing time slots for tasks, like a timetable. Both methods help us stay focused and make sure tasks actually get done

Eat That Frog means doing your hardest task first thing in the morning, when your energy is strongest. Time Blocking means fixing time slots for tasks, like a timetable, so you actually complete them instead of delaying

 “Time management is about using time wisely. Don’t try all techniques at once — just pick one, practice it, then add another slowly. Small daily habits make a big difference. Protect your hours, plan your priorities, and build a productive life.”
